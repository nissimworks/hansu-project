The silence

The knowledge does not retire. The room does.

If you retired recently, you know the silence that follows. For a few months the phone still rings. Then it stops. The title is gone, and with it the room where your experience was useful.

The experience itself is still there. Thirty years of decisions about people, money and timing do not disappear. What is missing is a place to put them.

That is the whole of what we are building: one founder, one advisor, a table, six months. Not a lecture. Not a committee. Not another part-time job.

You are not asked to teach. You are asked to look at one company with a founder and tell the truth about what you see.
What we ask of you

Four hours a month. The rest is our work.

You are not asked to run a class, write a report or join a committee. This is what the relationship actually asks of you.

  • Listen first. A founder usually knows the answer and needs to hear themselves say it.
  • Ask the question they are avoiding: who is the customer, why you, what happens if you do nothing.
  • Share what you lived through, including the decisions you got wrong. Those are worth more than the successes.
  • Open one door when it is real. A buyer, a former colleague, a supplier you trust.
  • Stay for six months. The value is in the memory, not the single meeting.

What you never do

  • No money changes hands, in either direction. Ever.
  • No investment in the founder's company during the program, and none for twelve months after it ends.
  • No hiring, and no legal, tax or investment advice as a professional service.
  • No use of your name in public without your written permission.
  • No confidential information leaves the pair without written consent.

What you get back

Being useful again, in a form you can keep

The strongest reason to do this is not charity. It is having a role that uses what you spent thirty years learning, with people who are still deciding.

A structure

Your experience becomes usable, instead of another vague offer to help. We make the introduction, hold the schedule and keep the record.

A peer group

The founding circle and the quarterly evenings: people who have run things, in a room without speeches.

Reverse mentoring

One hour a quarter where the founder shows you the tools, channels and habits replacing yours. The fastest way to stay current.

One person handles the rest

Scheduling, rooms, notes and problems that are not the conversation. Today that is one person, not a queue.

A record

A written account of what your advice changed, for you and for the program. Published only with permission.

No cost

No fee, no fundraising, no administrative work. And nobody will ask you for money.

The hard questions

The questions you are actually asking

A retired CEO would ask these before agreeing to anything. They deserve direct answers, including the ones that are not flattering to us.

Who is behind this?

닛심워크 (Nissim Work), a consulting company in Busan. It was founded and is run by Nofar Lupo, one person. There is no large institution standing behind this yet: no city office, no foundation, no list of partner organisations to show you.

What there is: a written structure, a set of rules, a named person accountable for the program, and a founding circle with three seats, two of them open. If that is not enough for you yet, we would rather you wait than join and be disappointed.

Is this paid, or am I expected to donate?

Neither. Advising is unpaid, and we never ask senior executives for money. At the founding stage the program is paid for by Nissim Work itself, and before you decide we will tell you exactly who pays for what.

Is this a startup pitch, or an investment scheme?

No. No money passes between you and the founder, no equity is involved, and you do not invest during the program or for twelve months after it ends. It is advice, not a deal. If anyone asks you for money in our name, tell us.

Why should I trust the matching?

You are not handed a name. Every senior executive goes through an application, a thirty-minute conversation, two references including at least one person who reported to you, and a written declaration of current board seats and investments. The founder applies in writing and meets you before either side commits. Both can end it at the three-month review, without an awkward conversation.

What happens if the founder does not listen, or it does not work?

Tell us. A founder who ignores advice is information, not a failure. We change the match, and you keep your relationship with the program. You will not have to manage the awkward part yourself.

What happens after six months?

The match ends on schedule. You decide whether to take another founder, join the founding circle if a seat is open, or step back. We will not promise you a permanent role before the program has proved it can keep one.

Will my name be used in public?

Not without your written permission. We do not publish a list of advisors, a testimonial or a photograph without asking you first, and a no is a complete answer.

I have been retired for a long time. Is my experience still relevant?

The tools have changed. Judgement about people, money and timing has not. That is what founders ask for, and it is the part that cannot be looked up.

Do I need to understand technology?

No. Half of what a founder needs is not technical: pricing, hiring, a customer who will not pay, a partner who will not sign. When the question is technical, we find a second advisor for that session.

Can I do this online?

Yes. The first meeting works better in person. After that, video or phone is fine.

How much of my time will this really take?

Four to six hours a month, including the note before each session, for six months. A four-hour onboarding comes once, before matching. There is no homework and no report to write.

Can I stop?

At any time, without explaining why. We will ask once whether something can be fixed, and then we will respect the answer.
