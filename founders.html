The gap

You learned to build. Nobody taught you to run.

Today's founders usually come from the technical side. You can build the thing. The work that decides whether it survives is the other half: who pays, what to charge, who to hire, which partner to trust, when to stop.

An engineering degree does not teach that. Neither does a grant application. It is learned from someone who has already made the mistake, and it is learned faster when that person is in the room while you decide.

More support, fewer mistakes.
What you get

What you get, and what it costs

What a founder receives is deliberately narrow. One person, one relationship, one record at the end. We would rather do this well for a few founders than promise a package to many.

01

One named advisor

Matched on your sector, your stage, the decision in front of you, temperament, location and language. One person for six months, not a rotating panel.

02

Six months, reviewed

Two ninety-minute sessions a month, with a review at month three. If the match is wrong, we change it without drama.

03

Introductions when they are real

A buyer, a former colleague, a supplier your advisor trusts. A door that opens because the person opening it is known at the other end.

04

A peer group

Cohort sessions with the other founders in your cycle, so the six months are not lived alone.

05

A written record

What you worked on and what changed, at the end. Useful for your next application, your next hire, or the next time you have to decide.

What we ask of you

Five commitments. Nothing else.

  1. Two sessions a month, ninety minutes each, for six months. Rescheduling is fine. Disappearing is not.
  2. A five-line note before each session: what changed, what is stuck, what you need.
  3. A one-page brief at the start: what the company does, the next three decisions, where you are stuck.
  4. Honesty about what is not working. An advisor who is told only good news cannot help.
  5. A short note at month six: what changed, even if the answer is nothing.
The rules that protect you

Written down, not implied

What your advisor never does

  • Invest in your company during the program, or for twelve months after it ends.
  • Pay you, take payment from you, or take equity of any kind.
  • Claim any part of your intellectual property.
  • Repeat what you say outside the pair without your written consent.
  • Give legal, tax or investment advice as a professional service.

You can end the match at any time, without explaining why, and stay in the program.

Questions

Asked by founders, answered plainly

Is it really free?

Yes. No fee, no equity, no success payment. At the founding stage the program is paid for by Nissim Work itself, so there is no outside funder whose terms you would be accepting.

I am not in Busan yet. Can I apply?

If you are registered in Busan, or moving here within three months, yes. Tell us the date in your application.

I am a foreign founder. Is the program in English?

Yes. Sessions run in Korean or English, and foreign founders are matched in the same way as Korean founders. The Navigator is bilingual too.

Will my advisor introduce me to investors?

Maybe, if the introduction is genuine. We do not promise fundraising, and an advisor who promises investment is breaking our rules.

What if my advisor does not understand my industry?

Tell us. Sector fit is one of six matching criteria, and changing the match is normal, not rude.

How much time will this take?

About four hours a month, including preparation. Less than most grant applications.

Who sees what I tell you?

The person who runs the program, and the advisor you choose. Your application is not published, and nothing about your company appears anywhere without your written consent.

What happens if it does not work?

Tell us at any point. We change the match or end it, and you stay in the program. There is a review at month three for exactly this reason.

Is this an accelerator?

No. No money, no desk, no demo day. One advisor, six months, and a tool that helps you find the public programs that do offer money, space and training.

Where is the program today?

At its founding stage. One person runs it. The founding circle has three seats for senior executives and two are open, and the first full cohort follows the pilot. Before you commit, we will tell you honestly who is seated and what the pilot will look like.
